Measure the sustainability of digital services

Digital services have environmental and social impacts across their lifecycle, from raw material extraction and processing, to manufacture, transport, operation and end-of-life. Measurement of the sustainability of digital services means to quantify these impacts.

Digital services have environmental and social impacts throughout their lifecycle.

These impacts occur during:

  • raw material extraction and processing
  • manufacturing
  • transport
  • operation
  • reuse, recycling and disposal
Measuring the sustainability of a digital service means quantifying its environmental and social impacts.

Impacts you can measure

The environmental and social impacts you can measure depend on the service, technology and lifecycle stage being assessed.

Environmental and social impacts across the digital lifecycle
Aspect Impacts
Environmental
  • Greenhouse gas emissions, such as carbon dioxide and methane
  • Freshwater use
  • Natural resource depletion
  • Land use change
  • Impacts on biodiversity
  • Pollution and electronic waste
Social
  • Human toxicity
  • Modern slavery risks
  • Labour rights violations
  • Social value

Defra's digital sustainability objectives

The Defra Digital Sustainability Strategy 2025 to 2030 sets out 6 strategic objectives. It includes key performance indicators for monitoring progress.

The objectives provide a framework for deciding what to measure.

  1. Reduce and mitigate carbon emissions in support of net zero targets.
  2. Reduce the wider environmental impacts of digital services, including water use and electronic waste.
  3. Reduce the use of natural resources and improve Defra's approach to the circular economy.
  4. Reduce social risks and deliver social value.
  5. Increase transparency and accountability within Defra and its supply chain.
  6. Improve the resilience of technology to climate and environmental risks.

Why you should measure impacts

Measuring environmental and social impacts helps teams:

  • identify where action is needed
  • prioritise improvements
  • make evidence-based decisions
  • track progress over time
  • check whether an improvement has been successful
Measurement should inform decisions and actions that improve a service. It should not be treated as an end in itself.

Measuring and reporting impacts also supports Defra's objective to increase the transparency and accountability of its digital footprint.

Choose a level of measurement

You can measure impacts at organisation, service or service-component level. Choose the level that supports the decision you need to make.

Levels of sustainability measurement
Level of measurement Purpose
Whole organisation

Demonstrates transparency and accountability to the public.

It can also show whether the organisation's long-term environmental and social strategy is working.

Whole digital service

Provides a benchmark that a service team can monitor and improve over time. For example, a team could measure greenhouse gas emissions per user or transaction.

Part of a service

Helps teams understand a specific part of the service, such as data storage, software, hosting or user devices.

Practitioners can use the results to identify improvements and assess whether their actions have been successful.

! Warning Define the scope, lifecycle stages, reporting period, units and assumptions before collecting data. This will help people interpret and compare the results correctly.
