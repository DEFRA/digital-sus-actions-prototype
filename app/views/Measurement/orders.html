Orders of impact for a digital service

Orders of impact of a digital project

The orders of impact for a digital service describe how its effects cascade outward from immediate functional changes to broader systemic or societal consequences.

There are three orders of effect relevant to measurement for ICT sustainability. Project and service teams should focus measurement on the first order impacts, as these are within the control of the service. Second order impacts require measurement against a baseline (such as an existing process or service which is being replaced) and assessing rebound effects, both which may be challenging. Third order impacts should be calculated at policy level.

Order Core Focus Nature of Carbon Impact
First-Order Infrastructure & Life Cycle Costly: The footprint of building and running digital systems.
Second-Order Application & Optimization Mixed: Carbon savings from efficiency vs. initial rebound spikes.
Third-Order Systemic & Behavioral Shift Mixed: Society-wide lifestyle changes, transforming global economic structures.

First order impacts

These are the direct environmental and social impacts from the lifecycle of ICT hardware and digital infrastructure. They represent costs to the environment and society, and include the impacts above such as carbon emissions, water, material use, e-waste and supply chain labour risks.

Second order impacts

These are the indirect environmental and social changes caused by how digital technology is applied to other sectors and processes. They can be positive (enabling efficiency) or negative (increasing demand).

For example, an assessment of the impact of a new service relative to what is replaced is calculating second order impacts.

Rebound effects are second order impacts. A rebound effect is the difference between the potential benefit and the actual impact. For example, a cloud provider creates new technology making data storage more efficient, and businesses respond by generating and storing more data such that the total footprint of data storage is now higher.

Third order impacts

These are medium- to long-term systemic shifts in economic structures, consumer culture, and human behaviour triggered by the permanent availability of ICT services.

In Defra these can be policy objectives, such as boosting recycling rates.
