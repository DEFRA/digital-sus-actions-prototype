Data availability, using proxies and direct measurement

Measurement gap

A ‘measurement gap’ exists in digital sustainability, whereby there is a disconnect between the fast-moving technology development (such as AI and cloud computing) and the tools available to track its real environmental and societal impacts.

Lack of access to quality data, application of consistent boundaries, methodologies and tooling mean that parts of digital systems are invisible, and comparison between services and products can be challenging.

Issue Description
Lack of access to quality data Data is often not made available from suppliers across supply chains, meaning parts of systems are often invisible. This leads to fragmented metrics and heavy reliance on rough estimates
No standard boundaries set for digital systems Making comparison between calculations difficult.
Tooling Tooling to enable teams to make calculations is often not available
Lack of industry-wide standard methodologies

There is no one universally adopted framework for measurement of IT systems and services, making comparisons between systems and services challenges. This is especially the case for AI. Existing frameworks include:

  • The Software Carbon Intensity (ISO Standard)
  • OECD AI Footprint initiative
  • GHG product standard
  • GHG protocol ICT guidance
Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up Divergence Macro or "top-down" estimates (extrapolating aggregate sectoral energy use) frequently clash with "bottom-up" calculations (summing individual device, component, and network footprints), leading to wide margins of error
Mandatory disclosure Methods, boundaries and assumptions are often not disclosed, making verification challenging

Direct measurement vs proxies

A proxy is an indirect measure that stands in for an environmental or social impact that cannot be measured directly. Changes to the proxy measure can demonstrate whether sustainability actions are having the intended effect, and support teams to prioritise their work. Project and service teams will often need to use proxies to estimate environmental impact, as direct measurement is not feasible.

! Warning Note that proxy data can be misleading, as it misses important differences.
Dropdown: volume of stored data as a proxy.
The volume of stored data, measured in gigabytes, can be used as a proxy for environmental impact. A reduction in stored data would indicate progress based on an assumption that less storage requires fewer resources and therefore has a lower environmental footprint. In this example, the proxy misses key distinctions such as the tier of storage the data is in. A larger volume of data held in ‘cold storage’ may have a lower carbon footprint than a smaller volume held in ‘hot storage’.

Proxies are often combined with a conversion factor to generate an estimate for energy, carbon or water usage. The formula for this is below. Activity data is the measurable unit of work, and the emission factor is a calculated consistent of the energy, CO2e or water is consumed per activity of work.

Environmental Impact = Activity Data (proxy) x Emission Factor
Dropdown: Carbon dioxide-equivalence as a proxy.

The global warming impact of a range of greenhouse gases (such as methane and nitrous oxide) are often converted into a figure of the warming impact that an equivalent amount of carbon dioxide would have. When we talk about ‘carbon’ emissions, this is carbon dioxide-equivalence, which is already a proxy measure.

https://www.theccc.org.uk/2012/05/16/getting-the-measure-of-ghg-emissions/

For digital systems, proxies are normally split into:

  • Spend based proxies
  • Activity based proxies

The proxy type selected will depend on what tooling and data is available to measure environmental and social impacts, and the level of accuracy required.

Spend based proxies

Spend-based proxies use monetary expenditure to measure environmental impact. This can either be done:

  • Using an emission factor (e.g., kg carbon dioxide per unit of currency) to estimate carbon emissions, or water or other environmental impacts.
  • Using cost as a directional indicator for resource consumption. For example, right sizing an over-provisioned resource or turning off in-active environments will save both cost and carbon.

There are instances where spend can be misleading. For example, newer and more efficient processers may cost more than older ones, while delivering lower energy consumption. Price fluctuation of cloud provider services can also occur independently to emissions making it an unreliable marker over time.

For cloud-based digital services, spend data is readily available through cloud billing APIs and standard invoices.

Pros Cons

Fast, low-effort, and highly scalable.

Easily pulled directly from internal procurement and accounting records.

Ideal for initial carbon hot-spot identification and baseline inventories.

Low granularity and lower accuracy.

Cannot distinguish between high-carbon and low-carbon suppliers or green products with the same cost

Distorted by inflation, currency fluctuations, and profit-margin variations rather than actual physical changes

Activity based proxies

Activity proxies are quantifiable metric of digital usage or activity. This could be:

  • computing hours (vCPU hours)
  • data storage
  • data transferred

[See DSA sections for software, architecture and data]

Activity data can also include offline aspects of the service:

  • distance travelled
  • fuel consumption
  • paper consumption

[See DSAs section on journey and process design]

Activity proxies can either be used as a stand in for environmental metrics directly or combined with conversion factors to estimate energy or carbon. Note that activity-based proxies may not capture important variables that influence the actual environmental impact,

For example, a reduction in the total volume of data transferred (measured in GB) over time may be used as an indicator of reduced environmental impact. Alternatively, data transfer volumes can be converted into estimated energy consumption and associated emissions using relevant conversion factors. What this wouldn’t capture is the such as the energy efficiency of the underlying network infrastructure, the efficiency of data transfer, or variations in local grid carbon intensity.

Selecting between direct measurement, spend based and activity based

Selecting between proxies and direct measurement means a trade-off between ease of calculation versus empirical accuracy.

Category Data examples Likely Accuracy Data Collection Effort
Energy measurement Metering of energy use High High
Activity-based Proxies Data transfer, vCPU hours Medium Medium
