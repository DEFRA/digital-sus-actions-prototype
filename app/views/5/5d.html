5d. Maximise efficiency in on-premises data centres

Take action to ensure that on-premises servers are running as efficiently as possible, reducing associated energy consumption To maximise efficiency in an on-premises data centre, through a focus on optimizing airflow, consolidating workloads, upgrading physical infrastructure, and tracking resource metrics.

An on-premises data centre is a physical computing facility owned, built, and managed directly by an organization within its own building or property

On premises systems give control over the hardware in a way that public cloud does not, Localized Control & Data Sovereignty, but can mean loweer hardware utilisation and over-provisioning as hardware required to cope with peak deamnd.

(i) Consolidate and virtualise workloads

Consolidating workloads means combining multiple applications, databases, or virtual machines that previously ran on separate physical servers onto a smaller number of higher capacity servers.

This approach can increase hardware utilisation rates and reduce overall energy consumption.

• Virtualisation: Deploy hypervisors and virtual machines (VMs) to raise typical server utilisation rates from low legacy baselines (10%–15%) up to 50% or higher, reducing the total number of physical machines needed. [1, 2, 3] • Retire Zombie Servers: Audit and shut down underutilised, abandoned, or single-purpose legacy servers that consume power without delivering active workloads. [1, 2] • Hardware Refresh Cycles: Replace older servers, storage arrays, and network switches with modern, energy-proportioned units that deliver higher compute-per-watt ratios. [1, 2, 3]

(ii) Implement dynamic balancing

Dynamic balancing involves distributing network traffic in real-time across local servers based on current demand.

This approach can optimise power distribution and reduce overall energy consumption.

(iii) Optimise airflow and cooling optimisation

• Hot and Cold Aisle Containment: Arrange server racks in alternating rows (hot-to-hot and cold-to-cold) and install physical barriers, curtains, or doors to prevent hot exhaust air from mixing with cool intake air. This can improve cooling efficiency by 20% to 50%. [1, 2] • Raise Operating Temperatures: Gradually increase room or server inlet temperatures within safe ASHRAE guidelines (saving roughly 4% to 5% in energy costs for every 1°F increase). [1, 2] • Advanced Cooling: Explore variable-speed fans, free-air cooling, or targeted liquid cooling for high-density hardware. [1, 2]

(iv) Power and Electrical Infrastructure

• High-Efficiency UPS and PDUs: Upgrade Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) systems and Power Distribution Units (PDUs) to high-efficiency models (aiming for online UPS efficiencies of 95%–98%). [1, 2] • Dynamic Power States: Enable built-in processor power management features (such as C-states and P-states) so hardware scales power draw down during low-demand periods. [1, 2] • Facility Lighting: Replace legacy fluorescent bulbs with low-energy LEDs tied to motion sensors. [1, 2, 3]

(v) Monitoring and Management

• Data Centre Infrastructure Management (DCIM): Implement DCIM tools to track real-time power, temperature, humidity, and rack-level usage. • Track Key Metrics: Regularly calculate and monitor Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) to measure how much total facility energy reaches the IT equipment versus overhead systems like HVAC.
