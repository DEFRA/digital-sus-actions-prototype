5b. Choose the lowest energy AI model that meets your needs

Lower-energy AI models reduce the carbon footprint of artificial intelligence by using smaller model architectures, compression techniques, and efficient inference strategies that can cut energy consumption.

READ MORE - some of these actions may be hard to do in practice. AI models may be integrated into other software packages etc. The industry is also at a relatively early point in identifying models, quantifying etc.

Likely co-benefits Cost: Lower energy AI models are often cheaper to operate

(i) Choose the smallest viable model

The size of an AI model refers to its scale and complexity, primarily determined by the number of parameters (the internal variables) that the model uses to process information and make predictions.

Resource consumption generally increases with the number of parameters a model has, as well as the tasks it asked to handle. Large models tend to be more computationally expensive to train and run, requiring specialized hardware like Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), small AI models can be trained for a specific task, are cost effective and they can be run 'at the edge'. This means these models can run on basic hardware (such as a PC or phone), saving greatly on resource consumption and greatly reducing the need for data transmission.

(ii) Choose an efficient model

Evaluate and compare models, to help you to choose more energy efficient models that provide functionality and outputs of acceptable quality and accuracy for your needs.

AI energy comparison tools, such as HuggingFace’s AI Energy Score Leaderboard, can help you choose models that are more energy efficient.

  • Green Software Foundation, Optimize the size of AI/ML models

(iii) Choose models with the minimum required 'modality'

‘Multimodal’ AI models can work with several types of data, such as images and text. However, the more 'modes' a model has, the higher the energy consumption tends to be.

Where possible, choose AI models with only the modality required for your project. For example, a chat-bot AI application will not need a model that can generate images. Studies have shown that generating images is significantly more energy intensive than text.

(iv) Re-use pre-trained models and transfer learning where possible

Fine-tuning an AI model is a process of adapting a pre-trained model to a new, specific task using a smaller, task-specific dataset.

Avoid training models where appropriate pre-trained models are available. Encourage transfer learning techniques and model sharing.
