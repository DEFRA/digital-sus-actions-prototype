6d. Take a more sustainable approach to operating AI

(i) Take a multi-tiered approach to AI

Rather than relying on a single solution, a multi-tier approach means choosing the right AI model for each task.

Implementing a multi-tiered approach requires that a suitable range of AI technologies (such as AI agents, Large Language Models and decision models) are available. User prompts and agent workflows can then be directed to the simplest and/or most cost-effective model capable of solving them reliably.

A multi-tiered approach reduces waste by avoiding simple prompts being sent to complex models, and in this way reduces energy consumption.

(ii) Optimise agentic AI workflows

Agents are autonomous AI systems pthat can set goals, make decisions, plan multi-step actions and use digital tools or APIs with minimal human supervision.

An AI agentic workflow is a multi-step process where agents make decisions, following a set of predefined rules and data inputs.

Agentic AI has the potential to automate complex processes and deliver significant efficiencies. However it also comes with significant resource consumption of its own.

Optimising the workflow that AI agents follow ensures that energy consumption is reduced as far as possible while output quality is maximised.

(iii) Implement prompt caching

Prompt caching stores the frequently unchanged first part of an AI prompt so the model does not have to reprocess those tokens on every request.

This approach can reduce input token usage by as much as 80%, lowering energy use and costs significantly

(iii) Implement token metering

Token metering is the process of tracking the number of tokens consumed when interacting with an AI model. This can cover both the ‘input’ tokens (the prompts provided for models) and ‘outputs’ that are generated as a response.

Metering tokens provides insight into opportunities for optimisation, and can enable intelligent model routing, where prompts are matched to smaller, task-specific models.
