6b. Choose the lowest energy AI model that meets your needs

Choise of models can have significant impact on the energy consumed by use of AI tech. Choosing the right model means understanding the task, and then considering the right-size model (as small as possible whilst still meeting capability requirements), modality, energy efficiency

(i) Choose smaller, task-specific models where appropriate (choose an appriopriately sized model)

Select the smallest viable model (for your task)

The size of an AI model refers to its scale and complexity, primarily determined by the number of parameters (the internal variables) that the model uses to process information and make predictions.

General-purpose AI models (such as Large Language Models) are those that have been trained on broad datasets, and can perform a variety of tasks, such as coding and generating text or images. In contrast, task-specific models are those built and fine-tuned for a single, well-defined function and trained on specific datasets.

General-purpose models are usually larger and more complex than task-specific models, with significantly more parameters. This leads to significantly higher consumption of resources during training and operational phases.

Choosing the smallest model that can deliver for the task at hand can lead to significant reductions to energy usage.

Smaller AI models require less computational power to run, and can sometimes be run 'at the edge' See Also run at the edge.

Co-benefits

Alongside sustainability impact, choosing smaller and task-specific models brings co-benefits in terms of:

  • Accuracy: Task-specific models often deliver higher domain accuracy and lower hallucination rates
  • Governance: Task-specific models make it straightforward to apply strict compliance and safety guardrails

(iii) Choose models with the minimum required modality

A 'Multimodal' AI model is a type of artificial intelligence that can process, interpret, and generate multiple types of data, such as text, images, audio, and video.

The more 'modes' a model has, the higher the energy consumption tends to be.

Where possible, choose AI models with only the modality required for your project. For example, a chat-bot AI application will not need a model that can generate images.

(iv) Take an 'open first' approach to model selection

An open-source AI model is one where underlying components, such as source code, model parameters, and documentation, are in the public domain.

If you want a general AI tool (rather than a more specialised model) then consider open source models.

Open source AI models are generally cheaper, and they often provide more transparency about performance and efficiency. There are a number of open-source models available, such as Mistral, Llama, DeepSeek or Hugging Face.
