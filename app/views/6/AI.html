5. Artificial Intelligence

While Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies can bring a wide range of benefits when used as part of digital services and other projects, they also have significant environmental impacts of their own.

There are a range of actions we can take to determine whether AI is right for a project and then to reduce the environmental impacts coming from the training and use of these technologies.

Artificial Intelligence and sustainability

Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies have a range of sustainability costs, but can also bring about benefits.

The 'direct impact', the environmental costs of AI can include things like:

  • significant energy use associated with the training and operation of AI
  • intense water demand for cooling the infrastructure (in data centres) used by AI
  • impacts from the mining, processing, manufacture and distribution required for the hardware used for AI models to run, as well as electronic waste produced when the hardware is disposed of
  • social impacts in terms of biases, exclusion and the impacts of training data filtering

Follow best practice from other sections on AI as well!

(i) Choose the greenest hosting solution "There are a range of factors that affect the environmental impacts of hosting solutions for AI: - Hosting that is sufficiently scalable and energy efficient - Located where there is renewable energy supply available on the grid - Located where there is good water availability, and systems for heat management and reuse - Situated close to users - Waste management strategies to prioritise the most circular options for disposal and recycling of old components and equipment - Transparent and regular reporting on the above See 'Hosting' theme for more details.

(i) Hardware

Pick energy-efficient hardware: Prefer ARM-based CPUs, inference-specific GPUs for better performance/watt Specialized Silicon: General-purpose CPUs are far less efficient for AI than purpose-built accelerators like GPUs, Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), and Neural Processing Units (NPUs).

Some AI Inference providers report on energy use and carbon metrics, " (ii) Follow best practice on data management when training models "AI datasets can have a large storage footprint. If you need to train a model, keep your data set as small as possible, avoid collecting unnecessary data and check if the data has already been collected elsewhere. Efficient storage of models also has significant implications for energy consumption. See Data section of these principles for more points on data best practice." (iii) Take a carbon-aware approach to training AI models "Being 'carbon aware' means adjusting workloads based on the real-time carbon intensity of the electricity grid. If you need to train a model, do this at off-peak times or overnight where possible, as demands on the energy grid will be lower at these times. You can also reduce the carbon footprint by training models in low-carbon regions, where this is possible."

    Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) for Web

    1. Using page weights as a proxy for carbon

    There are a number of methods, , that take the weight (in bytes) of web pages and uses this to estimate the carbon emissions from the transfer of this data to the end user. This means taking data transfer as an proxy indicator for total resource usage, and using this number to extrapolate energy usage as a fraction of the energy used by the total internet system. A number of online webpage carbon footprint calculators use these methods. This 'data transfer' approach is more appropriate for standalone websites rather than complex services, as it focused on the front end and does not consider what is going on on the ‘back end’, with hosting and databases for example.
