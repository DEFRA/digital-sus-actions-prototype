6a. Be clear on the need and readiness for AI use

Choosing the right tool for the problem at hand ensures the right outputs, but also can ensure that resource consumption is not unnecessarily high.

Ai technologies often come with significanrt resource consumption, associated with AI models require heavy GPU processing, may have much heavier resource consumption than traditional analytics or software.

Ensuring that there is clarity on the value that the use of AI technologies will bring to a project ensures that resources are not wasted, when an alternative approach could produce equal or better outcomes.

(i) Be clear on the use case for AI

Not every problem needs an AI technology as a solution.

For example, small or static datasets may not require complex machine learning or AI models to analyse. In this case, 'traditional' statistical methods may deliver the required result at a fraction of the resource consumption of AI technologies.

Consider whether AI is necessary for what you're trying to achieve and if so, which 'field' of AI is appropriate.

Different types of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies consume vastly different amounts of energy based on the model size, task complexity, and whether they are being trained or run in real time

Some questions to consider are:

  • What function will AI perform - will it collect data, automate tasks or generate new content?
  • What additional benefit does AI deliver compared to non-AI technologies or processes?
  • If AI is to be used, is generative AI required, or could an alternative such as a machine learning approach (e.g. a classifier model) be more appropriate?

From low to high energy use, a number of common AI technologies are:

Classifier model (machine learning) AI
Decision models AI decision models answers a question by selecting from a fixed set of answers rather than generating prose.

Co-benefits and trade-offs

Co-benefits

Alongside sustainability impact, avoiding unnecessary use of AI can bring co-benefits in terms of:

  • Reliability: Traditional analytics and automation approaches may deliver more transparent and predictable outputs
  • Operational overhead: Use of AI technologies may introduce additional compliance and auditing overhead over use of establised tools and processes
  • Speed: For smaller or static datasets, traditional approaches may produce results faster than using AI technologies

(ii) Check there is suitable data availability for AI use

AI needs data as its fundamental input to learn, identify patterns, and make predictions or decisions.

The quality and quantity of this data are crucial, as good data leads to more accurate and reliable AI performance. Poor quality data can lead to biased and flawed outputs, and may require a period of data cleaning and pipeline repair before it is ready to be used.

Make sure you have the data that is accessible, relevant and high-quality enough for your intended use of AI.

(iii) Create and maintain a register of AI use

An AI register is a catalog of the AI systems that a project or organisation is using.

A register can cover use cases, system types, ownership, data usage and track deployment, monitoring and compliance.

Transparency on what AI systems are being used across teams helps identify opportunities for resource optimisation and sharing and reuse of tools and datasets.

Co-benefits and trade-offs

Co-benefits

Alongside sustainability impact, creating a register of AI use can bring co-benefits in terms of:

  • Costs: An inventory of active AI systems can help identify where costs savings can be found through redundant tools that can be removed and avoiding duplicate purchases
  • Security: A register can help identify unsanctioned tools, and monitor security weaknesses
