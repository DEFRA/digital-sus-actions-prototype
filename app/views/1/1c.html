1c. Measure and report on environmental impacts

There are a number of approaches we can take to help ensure that users’ complete their journeys at the first attempt, avoiding the resources required to support in order to complete their goals with a service.

Read more

(i) Model the environmental impacts of different solutions under consideration

At the start of projects, a range of approaches or solutions may be under consideration.

For services following the Agile lifecycle, the Alpha phase specifically focuses on prototyping and testing different ideas in order to determine which to build in the Beta phase.

Modelling the environmental impacts of different solutions under consideration enables teams to better understand and factor sustainability into decision making about the best options to choose.

Where appropriate, modelling should include consideration of the systemic effects and impacts on behaviours, such as potential rebound effects from the use of technology.

(ii) Choose appropriate performance metrics

Measuring the environmental impacts of a service or project as it develops helps teams identify areas for improvement. To do so, we need to choose the right metrics. Metrics can focus on direct measurement or estimation of resource use or pollution, as well as proxy measures where appropriate. See the 'Measurement' guidance under each of the themes of the DSAs for more detail on approaching the choice of metrics. Measurement of the greenhouse gas emissions of digital services should follow the Greenhouse Gas Protocol ICT Sector Guidance where possible.

(iii) Include environmental sustainability in project planning

Non-functional requirements (NFRs) specify a software system's capabilities and constraints, such as speed, security, and reliability.

They dictate how a system behaves rather than what it does, and guide design and development choices as a project proceeds.

Projects should include sustainability in NFRs, which could include things like capping cloud carbon footprint per user transaction, setting minimum hardware lifecycles or scheduling certain types of workload for off-peak hours.

(iv) Ensure that climate risks are included in business continuity planning

Business continuity teams design, test, and execute plans to keep an organisation's core operations running during unexpected disruptions. These might include power outages, cyberattacks or the impacts of extreme weather events such as droughts.

Work with business continuity teams to ensure that appropriate and proportionate mitigation and adaptation plans are in place for climate resilience.
