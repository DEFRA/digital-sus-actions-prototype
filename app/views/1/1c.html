(i) Include sustainability in business cases

The Environmental Principles Policy Statement (EPPS) legally requires that ministers and policymakers give 'due regard' to 5 core environmental principles during policy development.

HM Treasury's Green Book explicitly integrates sustainability into business cases by embedding the duties of the Environmental Principles Policy Statement into the Five-Case Model. This means that projects are required to identify the potential environmental effects, either positive or negative, of options under consideration, and then apply the five EPPS principles to inform the design of the proposal.

Ensuring risks and benefits for environmental sustainability are sufficiently addressed in business cases helps to embed environmental objectives from the start or projects.

(ii) Include environmental sustainability in project planning

A project plan defines how a service or other project will be researched, designed, built, launched and maintained.

Environmental factors should be considered alongside other priorities to created a balanced project plan. This means considering:

  • Co-benefits between priorities, for example when a more lightweight digital service may bring performance, accessibility and inclusivity benefits alongside reducing energy consumption
  • Any trade-offs between envrionmental sustainability and other factors, for example if cost or the need to integrate with legacy systems may prevent the delivery of a solution with the lowest possible carbon footprint
  • Understanding the environmental risks of keeping parts of an existing service or process running alongside the new
