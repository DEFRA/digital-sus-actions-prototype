1b. Design lightweight digital pages, notifications and documents

Reducing the size, and processing of digital pages, notifications and documents reducing energy use and associated carbon.

Lead role families: Architecture Data
Likely co-benefits Cost: Lower energy AI models are often cheaper to operate

How to deliver this action

  • (i) Set page weight budgets
  • (ii) Design for text-based experiences
  • (iii) Take a lean approach to multimedia and interactive content
  • (iv) Design for lightweight and flexible notifications
  • (v) Choose lightweight publishing formats for documents

How to measure progress on this action

How to deliver on this action

The following points enable delivery of this action:

(i) Set page weight budgets

A page weight budget is an upper limit of the size of a webpage, measured in bytes.

Setting a limit on page sizes helps prevent bloat and saves on data transfer and energy usage.

Co-benefits

Alongside sustainability benefits, setting a page weight budget brings co-benefits in terms of:

  • Inclusivity: Budgets encourage close consideration of what is essential for users with small screen sizes or low bandwidth

(ii) Design for text-based journeys

Text-based user journeys experience by default to ensure all users can access core content

Designing for text-based journeys supports the delvelopment of services through the principle of 'progressive enhancement'

See also: 2b (i) Develop for progressive enhancement

Co-benefits and trade-offs

Co-benefits

Alongside sustainability benefits, developing software for progressive enhancement brings co-benefits in terms of:

  • Performance: Faster load times and reduced network usage, especially on low-bandwidth connections
  • Inclusivity: Services working on older devices, assistive technologies and for users with poor network connections or limited data allowances.

Trade-offs

Trade-offs from developing software for progressive enhancement may be:

  • Development Time: Requiring additional design and development effort to separate core and enhanced functionality
  • User Experience: Limiting use of complex, highly interactive front-end experiences

(iii) Take a lean approach to multimedia and interactive content

Multimedia content such as photos and videos, and interactive elements such as maps can add significantly to the 'weight' (the size in bytes) of a page.

Only use multimedia and interactive content when there is a clear user need, and allow users to make explicit choices to access multimedia and interactive content, rather than loading it by default.

See also: 2b Lazy loading

Co-benefits and trade-offs

Co-benefits

Taking a lean approach to multimedia brings co-benefits in terms of:

  • User experience: Pages load faster
  • Inclusivity: Pages with less mulitmedia and interactive content function better for users with slow connections

(iv) Design for lightweight and flexible notifications

Notifications like emails have a carbon footprint. This can increase when they have attachments. For services with many users these impacts can quickly mount. Take a lean approach to notifications by:

  • Only sending emails, SMS, website and other notifications when genuinely needed
  • Keeping notifications as lightweight (as small a file size) as possible
  • Setting up notifications so users can turn them off or choose how often to receive them

Measure progress on this action

Proxy measures

NB: The ‘data transfer approach’ to estimating digital carbon emissions

There are a number of web-focused methods, such as the ‘Sustainable Web Design Model’, that take the weight (in bytes) of web pages and uses this to estimate the carbon emissions from the transfer of this data to the end user. This means taking data transfer as an proxy indicator for total resource usage, and using this number to extrapolate energy usage as a fraction of the energy used by the total internet system. A number of online webpage carbon footprint calculators use these methods. Caveat: The 'data transfer' approach is more appropriate for standalone websites rather than complex services, as it focused on the front end and does not consider what is going on on the ‘back end’, with hosting and databases for example.
