1d. Design for reduced and lower-impacting offline steps of services

The offline parts of service journeys can include travel, printing paper documents and postage often represent significant sources of environmental impact for services. Consider opportunities to reduce the need for user travel and in-person interactions, whilst understanding when/if face-to-face human interaction maybe be more effective, or needed alongside digital touchpoints NOTE - this could include operations and support?

How to deliver on this action

We can adopt the following approaches in order to deliver on this action:

(i) Design with established patterns

Patterns can work at two levels with services:

  • A design pattern is a general solution to a common problem in software design, such as the layout of particular screen
  • A service pattern provides a solution to common service design problem or user task

Procurement of hosting

  • Will the service require new cloud hosting or data centre capacity?
  • If so, is sustainability being factored into the choice of locations for infrastructure, in terms of the proportion of renewable energy that is available and used on site, and levels of water stress? Are suppliers being selected that are monitoring and scoring well on sustainability metrics, as well as committing to manage and report on those at the service level?

Working with delivery suppliers

  • Will the project be working with suppliers to design and develop the service?
  • If so, have suppliers made commitments to environmental sustainability in delivery?

Planning for sustainable delivery from Private Beta

  • Are the team aware of and planning to follow best practice at Private Beta for the delivery of efficient, appropriately provisioned and carbon aware services in terms of software, AI, architecture, UX and journey design and data management?

Why it's important

Defra's services aim to bring tangible environmental benefits to our society – from improving water quality to boosting the nation's resilience to flooding. Benefits can also come from replacing existing processes that are relatively resource intensive with more lightweight and modern equivalents.

The use of technology for government services also contributes to climate change and other sustainability risks. This can include:

  • energy use that contributes to warming greenhouse gases
  • hardware manufacturing (mining and processing) that leads to water scarcity, resource depletion, and biodiversity loss
  • supply chains with unintended social impacts, such as labour exploitation

You can read more about the environmental impacts and Defra's 5 year digital sustainability strategy on GOV.UK (opens in a new tab).

What you need to do

Service teams should design, develop, and operate their services to be as sustainable as possible. For information on how to do this throughout your service's lifecycle, read the greener service principles guidance. The greener service principles are under review and updated guidance will be available later this year.

You will also need to set out your approach in a sustainability statement.

A sustainability statement should lay out what actions have been taken, and those that are planned, against the 6 objectives in Defra's digital sustainability strategy.
