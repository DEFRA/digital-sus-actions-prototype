(i) Encrypt data only when necessary

Encryption is a security method that takes data from a readable format and translates it into a code (or ciphertext) that can only be read via a 'key'.

Encryption can be a resource-intense process, and can increase data storage volumes as well as the energy consumed through encrypting and decrypting the data.

Take a risk-based approach by considering the sensitivity of data when determining whether encryption is really required.

Co-benefits and trade-offs

Co-benefits

Alongside sustainability impact, moving data to slower storage tiers brings co-benefits in terms of:

  • Performance: Helps optimise system performance by avoiding unnecessary encryption and decryption operations

Trade-offs

Potential trade-offs may include:

  • Speed: 'Cold' or archive storage tiers mean slower access times for data
  • Complexity: Moving data between tiers requires lifecyle policies to be defined and managed carefully

(ii) Choose appropriate cryptographic algorithms

Cryptographic algorithms are formulas that encrypt and decrypt data. Their energy consumption varies depending on factors like their complexity, application, and computing environment or platform.

Where possible, choose cryptographic algorithms that provide the necessary level of security whilst using no more energy than necessary.

(iv) Consider timing of security scans and updates

Reduces carbon emissions by shifting compute-heavy tasks to periods of lower grid carbon intensity.

Schedule intensive security scans and updates during off-peak hours to take advantage of lower-carbon electricity.
