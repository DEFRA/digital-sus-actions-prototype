Measure the environmental impact of data

Measuring the energy used to store and transfer data can help you find environmental impact hotspots and identify ways to reduce them.

You can include these estimates in a wider assessment of the environmental impact of a digital service or project.

The main measure is the energy used to store and transfer data. You can use this to estimate associated carbon emissions and water use.

Measuring data separately can help data architects and other practitioners track trends and assess whether changes to storage and transfer reduce environmental impacts.

Measure the energy used to store data

Data storage accounts for part of a data centre's energy use. Your estimate should include the electricity used by the storage hardware and a share of the energy used by supporting facilities, such as cooling equipment.

You should also identify the storage tier used for the data. Storage is commonly divided into hot, warm and cold tiers.

Typical characteristics of data storage tiers
Characteristic Hot tier Warm tier Cold tier
Typical technology Solid-state drives (SSDs) Hard disk drives (HDDs) Magnetic tape or offline disks
Access speed Very fast, around 10 to 200 microseconds Moderate, around 5 to 15 milliseconds Slow or sequential, from seconds to minutes
Power use Highest Moderate Lowest
Typical use Active databases, applications and real-time analytics General file storage and standard backups Long-term archives, compliance records, data lakes and disaster recovery

Storage tiers differ in cost, speed, reliability, power use and security. Choose a tier that meets the service's access, retention and resilience needs without using more resources than necessary.

The method you use will depend on whether the data is stored in the cloud or in an on-premises or externally managed data centre.

Estimate energy use for data stored in the cloud

Start with usage data from your cloud provider's billing or usage reports. These reports may include:

  • the total volume of data stored
  • the volume of data transferred
  • the storage tier used
  • the period covered by the figures

Some cloud providers also offer dashboards that estimate carbon emissions. Check what the dashboard includes before using its figures in a wider environmental assessment.

To estimate energy use, multiply the usage figure by an appropriate energy intensity factor.

Apply industry benchmark factors

You can use an industry benchmark when measured energy data is not available. The 2025 report Impact of Growth of Data Centres on Energy Consumption provides the following estimates.

Example energy intensity factors
Activity Indicative factor
HDD storage 0.0025 kWh per GB
SSD storage 0.008 kWh per GB
Uploading data to a data centre 0.02 kWh per GB
! Warning Benchmark factors are estimates. Record the source, date, units, assumptions and scope of every factor you use.

Other frameworks may use different units. For example, a capacity-time benchmark may express energy intensity in watt-hours per terabyte-hour. Do not compare or combine factors until you have converted them to compatible units and checked that their scopes match.

Use open-source tools

Open-source tools can apply energy factors to cloud usage data. Cloud Carbon Footprint classifies cloud usage and applies standard conversion factors.

Defra users can also assess whether Greenpixie can map items in a cloud Cost and Usage Report to physical hardware estimates. Its outputs can help estimate electricity use and Scope 3 emissions. Check the tool's methodology and coverage before relying on the results.

Account for time and storage state

Capacity-time factors require the volume of stored data and the length of time it is stored. Convert these figures into a unit such as terabyte-hours before applying the factor.

As a starting assumption:

  • Hot storage: assume that the storage is continuously available.
  • Warm storage: account for idle states and lower active use where the selected factor requires this.
  • Cold storage or tape: account for long periods when drives or tape libraries are powered down.

Use measured or provider-specific information instead of these assumptions when it is available.

Use financial cost as a proxy

You may not be able to identify the hardware used by a managed cloud service. In this case, some tools estimate energy use from billing data by applying an average energy-per-cost factor.

! Warning Treat cost-based estimates as a last resort. Prices can change without a corresponding change in energy use.

Account for data centre overhead

Multiply the storage hardware energy estimate by the provider's or facility's Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) value to account for supporting infrastructure such as cooling and power distribution.

You should also consider energy used for:

  • redundant or replicated copies of data
  • transferring data to and from the cloud
  • switching and routing data across networks

Check whether these activities are already included in the factor or tool you use. This will help you avoid counting the same energy twice.

Estimate energy use in on-premises and managed data centres

Use a bottom-up model when you have information about the storage hardware. Gather the following data:

Power demand
The measured or rated power used by the storage equipment. Use metered data where possible. Otherwise, use manufacturer data sheets.
Active time
The number of hours the equipment is powered on during the reporting period.
Capacity and utilisation
The installed capacity and the proportion used. Include the effects of compression and deduplication when reporting energy per effective terabyte of stored data.

If you know the equipment's total power demand, multiply it by its operating time to estimate energy use:

Energy use (kWh) = power demand (kW) × operating time (hours)

Use capacity and utilisation figures when you need to allocate part of the equipment's total energy use to a dataset, service or effective terabyte of storage.

Add data centre overhead

Multiply the hardware energy estimate by the data centre's PUE value to account for supporting infrastructure.

State whether your result includes only storage equipment or the full data centre overhead.

Calculate carbon emissions and water use

After estimating energy use, multiply it by an appropriate carbon intensity factor to estimate greenhouse gas emissions.

Use an appropriate water intensity factor to estimate water consumption. Carbon and water intensity factors vary by location, electricity source, provider and reporting period.

Record the factors, locations, dates and assumptions used in the calculation. Follow the wider measurement guidance for your service or organisation.
