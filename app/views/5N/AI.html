5. Artificial Intelligence

While Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies can bring a wide range of benefits when used as part of digital services and other projects, they also have significant environmental impacts of their own.

There are a range of actions we can take to determine whether AI is right for a project and then to reduce the environmental impacts coming from the training and use of these technologies.

Artificial Intelligence and sustainability

Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies have a range of sustainability costs, but can also bring about benefits.

The 'direct impact', the environmental costs of AI can include things like:

  • significant energy use associated with the training and operation of AI
  • intense water demand for cooling the infrastructure (in data centres) used by AI
  • impacts from the mining, processing, manufacture and distribution required for the hardware used for AI models to run, as well as electronic waste produced when the hardware is disposed of
  • social impacts in terms of biases, exclusion and the impacts of training data filtering

Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) for Web

1. Using page weights as a proxy for carbon

There are a number of methods, , that take the weight (in bytes) of web pages and uses this to estimate the carbon emissions from the transfer of this data to the end user. This means taking data transfer as an proxy indicator for total resource usage, and using this number to extrapolate energy usage as a fraction of the energy used by the total internet system. A number of online webpage carbon footprint calculators use these methods. This 'data transfer' approach is more appropriate for standalone websites rather than complex services, as it focused on the front end and does not consider what is going on on the ‘back end’, with hosting and databases for example.
