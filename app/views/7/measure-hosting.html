Measuring hosting impacts

Measuring the environmental impact of hosting requires that certain metrics for energy and water use are reported by data centre providers. These can then be factored into calculations for the energy, carbon and water use of a particular digtal service.

The ISO/IEC 30134 series is an international family of standards that defines standardized Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for measuring and reporting the operational resource efficiency and environmental impact of data centres.

Hosting providers can report at the following levels:

  • Facility-level
  • Service-level

Facility-level metrics

Facility-level metrics describe the environmental performance of the whole data centre, with metrics relating to energy use, as well as carbon and water.

These metrics can guide decisions on which data centre providers to choose through procurement, as well as being crucial to calculate data centre overhead (LINK) and convert energy use figures into estimates of carbon and water use (LINK).

Metric Description
Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) The ratio of the total energy used by a data centre with the energy used by its IT equipment. It is standardised in ISO/IEC 30134-2.
Renewable Energy Factor (REF) The ratio of renewable energy used by a data centre relative to its total energy consumption. Where possible this should separate renewable electricity generated on-site versus that purchased through Power Purchasing Agreements.
Cooling Efficiency Ratio (CER) The ratio of a cooling system's output cooling energy to its electrical input power
Energy Reuse Factor (ERF) The ratio of reused waste energy (such as server heat) exported outside the facility's boundary to the total energy consumed
Carbon Usage Effectiveness (CUE) The total carbon emissions generated by a data centre relative to the energy consumed by its IT equipment
Water Usage Effectiveness (CUE) The total volume of water used for facility operations (such as cooling and humidification) by the total energy consumed by IT equipment

Service-level metrics

Service-level metrics describe the share of environmental impact attributable to a particular cloud or hosting service.

Metric What to report
Total energy use Report absolute electricity consumption in kilowatt-hours (kWh), megawatt-hours (MWh) or gigawatt-hours (GWh) for the reporting period.
Total greenhouse gas emissions Report absolute emissions in tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO2e). Where possible, separate Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 emissions. Report location-based and market-based Scope 2 emissions separately.
Total water withdrawal and consumption Report absolute water withdrawal and consumption. Include the water source and information about local water stress where possible.
IT equipment utilisation Report the energy efficiency and utilisation of servers. Use the ITEEsv and ITEUsv metrics where appropriate.

Reporting on resilience

Reporting on the resilience of the Services and subcontractors to climate change, severe weather events (such as flooding and extreme temperatures) and ecological degradation, including:

  • Number of resilience risks identified affecting service continuity, infrastructure, supply chains and critical dependencies
  • Actions taken to improve resilience, adaptation and continuity plans
