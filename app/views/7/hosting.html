7. Hosting

Choices over hosting of computing infrastructure and data, whether data centres are public cloud or Data centre hosting impacts the environment primarily through heavy electricity consumption, massive water usage for cooling, and rapid electronic waste (e-waste) generation

  • On-Premises Data Centres: Private facilities built, owned, and operated by a single organisation for its own internal use
  • Colocation data Centres: Shared facilities where multiple organizations rent floor space, racks, or cages to house their own servers and hardware
  • Cloud Data Centres: Large, off-site facilities owned and managed by third-party cloud providers (such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, or Google Cloud).

The environmental impacts of data centres

>

Carbon: Driven by the data center's local energy mix (fossil fuels vs. renewables). For instance, server expansions in the U.S. alone project tens of millions of tons of additional CO₂-equivalent emissions annually. • Water: Massive amounts of fresh water are consumed by evaporative cooling systems in data centers. • Land: Physical infrastructure and data center land-use footprints

Also Also e-waste?

Also embodied impacts of hardware - the mining of materials etc.

Measurement

Data centre metrics

Cloud energy use

Metrics shall be calculated in accordance with the relevant current ISO/IEC 30134 standard, EN 50600-4 equivalent, or another recognised methodology.

Primary metric is Power Usage Effectiveness - PUE is the standard industry metric that measures how energy-efficient a cloud or data center facility is.

Other energy-related metrics

Other metrics that may be provided by hosting providers can include:

Metric Description
Carbon Usage Effectiveness (CUE) Measures the carbon footprint of a data center relative to the amount of computing work it performs
Renewable Energy Factor (REF) Measures the proportion of renewable energy used by a data center relative to its total energy consumption. Where possible this should separate renewable electricity generated on-site versus that purchased through Power Purchasing Agreements.
Cooling Efficiency Ratio (CER) The ratio of a cooling system's output cooling energy to its electrical input power
Energy Reuse Factor (ERF) The ratio of reused waste energy (such as server heat) exported outside the facility's boundary to the total energy consumed

Water Usage Effectiveness

Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) is calculated by dividing the total volume of water used for facility operations (such as cooling and humidification) by the total energy consumed by IT equipment over a given period. WUE should be calculated in in accordance with ISO/IEC 30134-9 or EN 50600-4-9
