7d. Choose providers with proportional resilient infrastructure

The UK government addresses infrastructure vulnerability through the GOV.UK Data Centre Covenant, requiring annual reporting on energy and water efficiency, emissions, and grid flexibility, alongside the House of Commons Library Research Briefing on Data Centres which notes data centers were designated critical national infrastructure

Resilient digital services: it can withstand disruptions, maintain continuous operations, and recover quickly from failures

Resilience measures come with their own environmental impacts.

Resilient public services are those designed to withstand and rapidly recover from the impacts of these events, as well as cyber attacks and others threats, ensuring that there are no significant breaks in service availability for citizens. However, the flipside to the question of resilient digital public services is the environmental impact of resources we put into backups and redundancy, and that is a trade-off we need to carefully strike. As Mark Butcher described it, appropriate resourcing for digital services is not something that the industry as whole has gotten right to this point: ‘We are wasting half of every pound we spend on IT’ at present, building services that are ‘over-sized and over-resourced.’ In order to make informed decisions about the appropriate levels of redundancy and resilience for public services, he argued ‘we need better data on risks, carbon and usage, so we can understand wastage.’

Resilience plans for data centres are strategies that are designed to keep facilities operating continuously (or recover them rapidly) when events like power failures, cyberattacks, natural disasters, or equipment breakdowns occur.

The supplier has or commits to implementing physical and non-physical solutions (‘adaptation solutions’) that substantially reduce the most important physical climate risks that are material to the service.

Choose providers with robust resilience plans that include climate events

(i) Choose providers incorporating resilience into site selection

Factoring resilience into site selection means choosing geographical locations that minimise exposure to environmental and logistical risks while maximising access to critical resources

For data centres, this looks liks:

Aspect Description
Map climate risks at the site level Use hyper-local analysis to map risks of flooding, high temperatures, water scarcity, earthquakes, or wildfires
Scenario testing Model multiple time horizons and high-emissions climate trajectories during the pre-construction phase to identify critical points where equipment or cooling will fail
Power Grid Stability & Capacity Ensuring reliable, high-capacity electricity connections, access to renewable energy sources, and proximity to substations to prevent grid-failure blackouts
Geographic Redundancy Distribute mission-critical services across geographically decoupled zones to prevent single-region failures during correlated climate events

(ii) Proportionate data centre resilience design for physical and technical systems

Resilient data centre design means engineering a facility’s physical and technical systems so it can withstand disruptions, maintain continuous operations, and recover quickly from failures.

Choose hosting providers with proportionate resilience measures to avoid excess energy waste while maintaining resilience.

Aspect Description
On-site generation and storage Dedicated backup generators, Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS), and co-located renewable energy or battery storage systems to ensure that critical servers and services do no go offline if there is a power failure
Heatwave-adapted systems Cooling infrastructure such as liquid cooling, closed-loop systems, or higher-capacity chillers to handle sustained heatwave temperatures, and analytics in place to aid dynamically adjust workloads during sudden temperature spikes to avoid overheating
Water-scarcity redundancy If data centres are located in water-stressed regions, transition away from water-intensive evaporative cooling methods or install on-site water recycling and storage
Physical and structural engineering Build structures to withstand wind speeds during storms, and elevate physical infrastructure above predicted flood lines

(iii) Operational side of things

Operational Preparedness and Supply Chain for climate resilience, Active Emergency Response & Early Warning

Aspect Description
Early-warning systems for climate events Integrate real-time weather tracking and natural catastrophe monitoring tools into site management workflows
Emergency Response Planning Establish clear protocols for staff safety, site accessibility during floods or wildfires, and emergency fuel/water resupplier contracts
Upstream dependencies Secure backup paths for fibre-optic routes, transport corridors, and cloud dependencies so a local physical impact does not sever broader network operations
