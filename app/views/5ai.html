Artificial Intelligence and sustainability

Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies have a range of sustainability costs, but can also bring about benefits.

The 'direct impact', the environmental costs of AI can include things like:

  • significant energy use associated with the training and operation of AI
  • intense water demand for cooling the infrastructure (in data centres) used by AI
  • impacts from the mining, processing, manufacture and distribution required for the hardware used for AI models to run, as well as electronic waste produced when the hardware is disposed of
  • social impacts in terms of biases, exclusion and the impacts of training data filtering

Services incorporating AI are usually more intensive to run than other digital services, so it is especially important to consider this environmental impact before deciding whether to deploy or use AI.

AI technologies can bring about environmental benefits to offset the costs: improve the efficiency of existing processes, and speed up the delivery of government services

Defining Artificial Intelligence

The Artificial Intelligence Playbook for the UK Government uses the definition of an AI system adopted by OECD countries:

'An AI system is a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments. Different AI systems vary in their levels of autonomy and adaptiveness after deployment.'

Measurement and tracking progress

Estimating the energy, carbon and water use for the AI system over its lifecycle requires transparency and explainability about the AI model itself, the hardware used to train it, and how and where it is currently being hosted.

The Software Carbon Intensity metric (SCI) for AI extends the globally adopted Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) ISO specification to address the unique characteristics of artificial intelligence systems. It provides a standardized methodology for calculating carbon emissions rates across the entire AI lifecycle, from data preparation and model training to deployment and inference.
