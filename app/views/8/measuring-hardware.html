Measuring the impact of hardware

There are a range of metrics that can be provided to demonstrate the environmental and social credentials of hardware that is rented, purchased, monitored and retired.

To measure the sustainability of purchased ICT hardware, the strongest approach is a whole-lifecycle assessment covering manufacture, use, lifetime extension and end-of-life, energy efficiency metrics, and circularity indicators outlined in frameworks like the UNDP Sustainable Procurement of ICT Equipment Guide and the UK Government's Circular Economy Working Group Hardware Lifecycle Management Toolkit

New devices

Ecolabel certifications

Ecolabel certification ensures coverage of a wide range of environmental and social issues through a single externally assured standard which is kept up to date over time.

Type I ecolabels contain clearly defined third-party assured criteria for devices, and should conform to ISO 14024. Type II ecolabels are self-declared environmental claim and must conform with ISO 14021 standards.

Organisations can report on:

  • Number of new devices meeting Type I ecolabels (such as EPEAT or TCO) purchased or rented
  • Number of new devices meeting Type II ecolabels purchased or rented

Sustainability Impact Reporting and Lifecycle Assessments

Alongside ecolabels, hardware devices may be assessed by sustainability impact report or Life Cycle Analysis. These can cover a range of environmental performance and impact metrics such as:

  • estimated power consumption (in modelled scenarios of idle, active & rated power usage)
  • embodied carbon figures
  • embodied water (m3)
  • proportion and weight of recycled materials
  • use of critical minerals
  • energy efficiency certification;
  • weight of packaging
Metrics should be calculated in accordance with ISO 14040 & 14044 or another recognised methodology.

Second life devices

Quality assurance for second life devices can be demonstrated through third-party certifications, such as:

  • ISO 9001 (Quality Management Systems)
  • BS 8887‐220:2010 (remanufactured products)
  • BS 8887-240:2011 (reconditioned products)
  • PAS 141:2011 (reuse of UEEE and WEEE)
  • BS EN IEC 62368-1:2024+A11:2024 (ICT Safety Requirements)

Monitoring device performance

Monitoring the performance of hardware can cover new and second life devices.

  • energy consumption by devices​ (Kwh)
  • the Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions from devices
  • the number of devices extended beyond standard refresh cycle
  • the number of refurbished and remanufactured devices deployed
! Warning Energy consumption reporting should be based on actual monitored usage. GHG emissions must use location-based calculations (not market-based) and should include scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions.

End of life

Disposal of hardware should follow the waste hierarchy, setting out how devices are repaired, refurbished, re-used or donated before they are recycled.

Reporting at end of life can cover the volume of hardware:

  • repaired
  • refurbished
  • redeployed
  • reused externally (split into donation & sold)
  • recycled
  • landfilled (split into with & without energy recovery)
  • incinerated
  • weight of components recovered for reuse and materials (including critical minerals) recovered for recycling
Reporting should also confirm that secure data sanitisation or destruction has been completed in accordance with security policies.
