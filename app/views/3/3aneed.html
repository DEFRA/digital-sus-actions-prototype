(i) Be clear on the use case for AI

Not every problem needs an AI solution. Consider whether AI is necessary for what you're trying to achieve, what the benefits are that will come from AI use, and if there's a simpler approach that would work just as well.

Questions to consider are:

  • What function will AI perform - will it collect data, automate tasks, generate new content?
  • Is generative AI required, or could non-generative AI such as image recognition or natural language processing be more appropriate?
  • What additional benefit does AI deliver compared to non-AI technologies or processes?

(ii) Check there is suitable data availability for AI use

AI needs data as its fundamental input to learn, identify patterns, and make predictions or decisions. Machine learning models are trained on large, task-specific datasets like text, images, or sensor readings. The quality and quantity of this data are crucial, as good data leads to better, more accurate, and reliable AI performance, while poor data can result in flawed models and outputs.

Make sure you have the data that is accessible, relevant and high-quality enough for your intended use of AI.

(iii) Assess the likely environmental impact of the use of AI

Service teams should design, develop, and operate their services to be as sustainable as possible. For information on how to do this throughout your service's lifecycle, read the greener service principles guidance. The greener service principles are under review and updated guidance will be available later this year.

You will also need to set out your approach in a sustainability statement.

A sustainability statement should lay out what actions have been taken, and those that are planned, against the 6 objectives in Defra's digital sustainability strategy.
