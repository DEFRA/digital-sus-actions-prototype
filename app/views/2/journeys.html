2. Journey & Process Design

Service journeys should be designed so users can complete their goals while using as few resources as possible. This approach should cover the ‘end-to-end’ journey, including both online and offline channels, as well as considering both external users and internal staff.

Digital channels

Offline channels

User effort proxies (from analytics and research)

  • Time on task for registration and data submission
  • Completion rate on first attempt
  • Sessions per completed submission, as a measure of abandoned or repeated visits.

Regulator and operator effort proxies (from service data and casework)

  • Support contacts per hundred registrations, by reason, showing avoidable rework.
  • Completion rate on first attempt
  • Sessions per completed submission, as a measure of abandoned or repeated visits.
• Proportion of submissions accepted without regulator intervention, versus those needing a query or manual correction. • Volume of duplicate registrations and duplicate evidence notes caught or created. • Paper and email handled outside the service, for example signed declarations or spreadsheets sent to regulators.
