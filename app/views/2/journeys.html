2. Journey & Process Design

Decisions about user journeys and business processes can have very significant environmental impacts , because they determine how many activities, transactions, journeys, devices and physical resources are needed to achieve an outcome.

Service journeys should be designed so users can complete their goals while using as few resources as possible. This approach should cover the ‘end-to-end’ journey, including both online and offline channels, as well as considering both external users and internal staff.

Journey design and business process design are two complementary frameworks used to plan how an organization operates and how people interact with it, differing primarily in their starting point and scope.
  • Journey Design is an outside-in approach that maps and optimizes the holistic, end-to-end experience a customer, user, or employee has with a brand across time and touchpoints. It focuses on human emotions, mindsets, pain points, and non-linear behaviours from discovery to loyalty.
  • Business Process Design is an inside-out approach that structures, sequences, and standardizes internal operational steps, resources, rules, and technology systems. It focuses on efficiency, resource optimization, risk control, and predictable output
