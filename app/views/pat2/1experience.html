Design and sustainability

'An AI system is a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments. Different AI systems vary in their levels of autonomy and adaptiveness after deployment.'' [Definition from the OECD]

Measuring impacts

Services incorporating AI are usually more intensive to run than other digital services, so it is especially important to consider this environmental impact before deciding whether to deploy or use AI.

  • significant energy use associated with the training and operational of AI
  • intense water demand for cooling the infrastructure (in data centres) used by AI
  • impacts from the mining, processing, manufacture and distribution required for the hardware used for AI models to run, as well as electronic waste produced from the hardware required for AI use
  • social impacts in terms of biases, exclusion and the impacts of training data filtering

See more in the Defra Digital Sustainability Strategy and the Defra AI Position Statement.

Measurement

Estimating the energy, carbon and water use for the AI system over its lifecycle requires transparency and explainability about the AI model itself, the hardware used to train it, and how and where it is currently being hosted.

The Software Carbon Intensity metric (SCI) for AI extends the globally adopted Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) ISO specification to address the unique characteristics of artificial intelligence systems. It provides a standardized methodology for calculating carbon emissions rates across the entire AI lifecycle, from data preparation and model training to deployment and inference.
